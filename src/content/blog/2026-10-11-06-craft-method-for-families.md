You’ve had the big talk more times than you can count. You’ve pleaded, you’ve threatened, and you’ve gone quiet for a week to “let them figure it out.” Nothing has really changed, except that everyone in the house is more tired and more on edge.

If that’s where your family is, you may have heard about the CRAFT method for families and wondered if it’s one more idea that sounds good on paper. It isn’t. CRAFT gives families a kind, practical way to keep influencing a loved one who isn’t ready for help, without fighting and without walking away.

This guide explains what CRAFT is in plain words, why it tends to work better than confrontation or detachment alone, the core skills, and how your whole family can use it together.

## What is the CRAFT method for families?

CRAFT stands for Community Reinforcement and Family Training. It teaches family members to change how they respond to a loved one’s drinking or drug use, so that sober choices feel more rewarding and using feels less comfortable.

CRAFT was developed by addiction researchers and has been tested with real families, so it rests on evidence rather than opinion or tradition. It has three goals, and they matter equally:

- Helping your loved one cut back or stop using.
- Helping your loved one become willing to start treatment.
- Helping you and the rest of the family feel better, whether or not your loved one changes.

The idea underneath it is simple: people keep doing what pays off for them. Right now, using pays off for your loved one in some way, such as relief, fun, escape or belonging. CRAFT helps the people closest to them make a sober life pay off more.

## Why does CRAFT work better than confrontation or detachment alone?

Because it keeps you connected and uses that connection on purpose. Confrontation tends to make a person defend their use, and detachment alone steps back from the relationships that have the most pull.

Lectures, ultimatums and surprise showdowns usually trigger shame. A person who feels cornered argues, leaves, or uses to escape the feeling. Even when confrontation gets a promise, the promise rarely lasts, because it was made under pressure.

Detachment has real value. Learning that you didn’t cause this and can’t control it is a huge relief, and groups like Al-Anon and Nar-Anon help many families heal. But detachment alone can leave you feeling like your only job is to wait. CRAFT keeps the self-care and adds everyday skills that give you real influence.

When researchers have compared these approaches, CRAFT has helped more families get a resistant loved one to start treatment than confrontation or support-group participation alone. And in more than 20 years of working with families, the pattern I see most often is a family taking turns: one person yells, another rescues, a third gives up. CRAFT breaks that cycle by giving everyone the same playbook.

## What are the core skills of CRAFT?

There are five: [positive communication](/blog/talk-about-drinking-without-fighting), rewarding sober time, allowing natural consequences, taking care of yourself, and [inviting your loved one to treatment](/blog/when-loved-one-is-ready-for-treatment) at the right moment. All five work better once you understand your loved one’s pattern.

### First, learn the pattern

Notice when, where and with whom your loved one usually drinks or uses, what tends to happen right before, and what they seem to get out of it. A father might realize his son almost always uses after a shift at a job he hates, with the same two friends. That shows you where the pressure points are and where a sober alternative could fit.

### 1. Positive communication

Say what you want more of, not only what you’re sick of. Keep it short, start with something kind, and speak about your own feelings. Instead of “You ruined another night,” try: “I loved having dinner with you on Tuesday. I’d really like more nights like that.”

### 2. Rewarding sober time

When your loved one is sober, make that time warm and worth having: a favorite meal, a drive, a game, your full attention. You aren’t bribing them. You’re making sure sobriety feels like connection, not punishment. When they’re using, step back politely: “I’m going to head to bed. I’d love to watch the game with you tomorrow.”

### 3. Allowing natural consequences

Stop absorbing the fallout of their use. Don’t call in sick for them, cover the overdraft, or explain away the missed family party. You aren’t adding punishments; you’re simply no longer removing the problems their use creates. The one exception is safety. Allowing consequences never means leaving someone in danger.

### 4. Taking care of yourself

CRAFT treats your well-being as a goal, not an afterthought. Rest, friends and support of your own keep you steady enough to use the other skills, and a calm family member is far more persuasive than an exhausted one.

### 5. Inviting your loved one to treatment

CRAFT teaches you to watch for moments when your loved one is more open, such as after a painful consequence or during a stretch of regret, and to invite them then, with a specific option already lined up. A short, warm invitation at the right moment does more than a long argument at the wrong one.

## How can your whole family use CRAFT together?

By agreeing on the same responses, so your loved one gets one clear message instead of five different ones. CRAFT loses much of its power when one parent rewards sober time and the other hands over cash on a bad night.

Start with a family meeting without your loved one, and work through these questions together:

- What will each of us do when they’re sober, and what will each of us do when they’re using?
- Which consequences will we stop absorbing, and who is most tempted to step in?
- Who will extend the invitation to treatment when the time comes, and how?

## How do you take care of yourself and stay safe while using CRAFT?

Make your own steadiness part of the plan, and put safety ahead of every other skill.

- Get support for yourself. Many counselors and interventionists are trained in CRAFT, and support groups help you stay grounded.
- Expect slow, uneven progress. Measure your success by what you do, not by what your loved one does this week.
- If your loved one decides to stop drinking or using sedatives, encourage them to see a doctor first. Withdrawal from alcohol, benzodiazepines or other sedatives can be dangerous.
- If opioids are involved, keep naloxone (Narcan) at home. It’s available without a prescription at most U.S. pharmacies.

If your loved one has ever threatened, hurt or controlled you, your safety comes first. Don’t confront them or give an ultimatum without a safety plan, and consider calling the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. If anyone is in immediate danger or you suspect an overdose, call 911. For a mental health or suicide crisis, call or text 988. For free, confidential help finding treatment or family support, SAMHSA’s National Helpline is 1-800-662-4357, available 24/7.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the CRAFT Method for Families

### Does my loved one have to know we’re using CRAFT?

No. CRAFT is designed for family members to use on their own, and your loved one doesn’t need to agree or take part. You aren’t tricking them; you’re changing how you respond. Some families choose to be open about it, and that’s fine too.

### Isn’t rewarding them just enabling?

No. Enabling means protecting someone from the effects of their use, which makes it easier to keep going. CRAFT does the opposite: it rewards sober behavior and lets the consequences of using land where they belong.

### How long does CRAFT take to work?

There’s no set timeline. Many families notice the house feels calmer fairly soon, simply because there’s less fighting. Your loved one’s willingness to get help moves on its own schedule, so steady effort matters more than speed.

### Does CRAFT work for both alcohol and drugs?

Yes. Families use CRAFT whether their loved one is drinking, misusing prescription drugs or using illegal drugs. The skills stay the same; what changes is the safety planning, such as keeping naloxone at home when opioids are involved.

## Your Family Doesn’t Have to Figure This Out Alone

CRAFT works best when the people who love someone stop pulling in different directions and start pulling together. You don’t have to master every skill this week. Pick one, agree on it as a family, and practice it.
